01 · Project context

Many small printing labs, one unmet demand — no system connects them

The supply

Independent FFF/SLA labs — the university maker lab plus a confirmed partner workshop — each with idle machines, declared capacities and their own queues managed over chat apps.

The demand

Students, researchers and small businesses want parts printed to a date they can trust, at a price that does not change per lab.

The thesis

Treat the whole network as one virtual factory: uniform pricing, capacity-based delivery promises, central quality control at a hub — decided by software, not WhatsApp.

02 · Problems to solve

Five failures of the chat-group print shop

P1

Idle machines beside refused orders

Capacity is fragmented per lab; nobody sees the whole network, so jobs queue at one machine while three sit idle two buildings away.

P2

Delivery dates are guesses

Quotes promise "3–5 days" from a static table. Without trial placement against real schedules, commitments are fiction and late parts are normal.

P3

Quality drifts with no accountability

No inspection standard, no fault attribution between lab, hub and model file — defects become he-said-she-said instead of a reprinted part.

P4

Handoffs and disruptions are manual

Declines, failed prints, breakdowns are negotiated person-to-person; a disruption mid-run costs hours of chat, not seconds of replanning.

P5

Price depends on who asks which lab

Per-lab haggling destroys trust in the network brand; prices must come from one versioned formula, identical for every customer.

What success looks like

One real-money order travels upload → quote → pay → assign → print → QC → ship → delivered with zero manual dispatch, and a disruption is repaired in ≤ 30 s.

02b · The mechanism that answers them

Four pillars, one core contribution

Network-uniform pricing

One formula over versioned parameter sets; the version is frozen onto every quote — change the rates tomorrow, yesterday's quote still reproduces. FR-SCHED-010

Speculative quote-time scheduling ★ core

At quote time the engine runs a trial placement on the real machine timelines and returns the earliest feasible date (with rush surcharge). The promise is computed, not looked up. FR-SCHED-005

Hub-mediated quality

Parts route through a central hub: batch reconciliation, grade-bound checklists, defect taxonomy with fault attribution, reprint inheriting the original deadline. FR-HUB-001…005

Event-driven rescheduling

Decline, print failure, breakdown, QC-fault each trigger plan repair within a 30 s hard budget with a committed fallback — in-progress jobs untouched. FR-SCHED-007

Customers never see which lab prints their part; labs never see each other's prices. Uniformity is enforced by architecture, not policy.
