# Stakeholders - PrintGrid Platform

## 1. Primary Stakeholders

### 1.1 Customers (End Users)
**Who They Are:**
- Individuals needing custom 3D printed parts
- Small businesses requiring prototypes or small-batch production
- Designers and engineers with one-off fabrication needs
- Hobbyists and makers with specialized projects

**Their Needs:**
- Competitive, transparent pricing
- Reliable delivery dates
- Consistent quality standards
- Easy upload and configuration process
- Real-time order tracking
- Intellectual property protection for their designs

**Their Pain Points:**
- Uncertainty about which lab can fulfill their requirements
- Inconsistent pricing across different labs
- Difficulty comparing quality and capabilities
- Time wasted getting multiple quotes
- Risk of missed deadlines

**Success Metrics:**
- Order completion rate
- On-time delivery rate
- Customer satisfaction scores
- Repeat purchase rate

---

### 1.2 Printing Labs (Service Providers)
**Who They Are:**
- University fabrication labs (FabLabs)
- Community maker spaces
- Small commercial 3D printing shops
- Independent printing studios
- Technical workshops with 3D printing capabilities

**Their Needs:**
- Steady stream of work matched to their capabilities
- Fair compensation for their services
- Clear production schedules and due dates
- Visibility into their performance metrics
- Protection from scope creep and unrealistic demands

**Their Pain Points:**
- Inconsistent order flow (feast or famine cycles)
- Customer negotiations and service disputes
- Difficulty filling capacity during slow periods
- Risk of accepting work beyond their capabilities

**Success Metrics:**
- Machine utilization rate
- Revenue per month
- On-time completion rate
- Customer satisfaction (via platform)
- Defect rate

---

### 1.3 Lab Operators (Production Staff)
**Who They Are:**
- Technicians running the 3D printers
- Students working in university labs
- Maker space volunteers and staff
- Print shop employees

**Their Needs:**
- Clear job queue and priorities
- Easy-to-use production interface
- Ability to report issues quickly
- Access to model files when needed
- Straightforward handover procedures

**Their Pain Points:**
- Unclear priorities when multiple jobs are queued
- Difficulty reporting production issues
- Unrealistic deadlines from poor planning
- Lack of visibility into why jobs are assigned to them

**Success Metrics:**
- Jobs completed per day
- Actual vs. estimated print time accuracy
- Incident report response time
- Interface usability score

---

### 1.4 Hub Quality Control Staff
**Who They Are:**
- Quality inspectors at the central hub
- Technical staff trained in 3D print defect identification
- Specialists in different 3D printing technologies

**Their Needs:**
- Clear inspection checklists per quality grade
- Ability to classify defects accurately
- Tools to attribute faults to the correct party
- Streamlined reprint workflow
- Photographic evidence management

**Their Pain Points:**
- Ambiguous quality standards
- Disputes over who caused a defect
- Difficulty tracking which lab produced which part
- Lack of feedback loop to labs about quality issues

**Success Metrics:**
- Inspection throughput (parts per hour)
- First-pass yield rate
- Defect attribution accuracy
- Reprint turnaround time

---

### 1.5 Hub Fulfillment Staff
**Who They Are:**
- Warehouse and logistics personnel
- Packing and shipping specialists
- Inventory management staff

**Their Needs:**
- Clear order consolidation workflow
- Tracking of incoming lab deliveries
- Integration with shipping carriers
- Accurate order completion status

**Their Pain Points:**
- Parts arriving at different times for same order
- Missing or misidentified items
- Packing complexity for mixed orders
- Shipping deadline pressure

**Success Metrics:**
- Order consolidation time
- Packing accuracy rate
- Shipping on-time rate
- Damage rate during fulfillment

---

### 1.6 Operations Manager
**Who They Are:**
- Platform operations lead
- Production manager overseeing the network
- Escalation point for critical issues

**Their Needs:**
- Real-time network visibility
- Ability to intervene on problematic orders/labs
- SLA monitoring and reporting
- Performance analytics across the network
- Tools to manage lab standing and capacity limits

**Their Pain Points:**
- Discovering issues after they've escalated
- Lack of visibility into network bottlenecks
- Difficulty balancing customer promises with lab capabilities
- Manual intervention slowing down operations

**Success Metrics:**
- Network utilization rate
- SLA compliance rate
- Average response time to critical issues
- Manual intervention frequency (should decrease over time)

---

### 1.7 Platform Administrator (System Admin)
**Who They Are:**
- Technical administrator
- Configuration manager
- Security and access control officer

**Their Needs:**
- User and role management tools
- Configuration interface for business parameters
- Audit log visibility
- System health monitoring
- Ability to update catalogs and parameters without deployment

**Their Pain Points:**
- Configuration changes requiring code deployment
- Difficulty tracking who changed what and when
- Security incidents requiring forensic investigation
- System performance issues

**Success Metrics:**
- System uptime
- Configuration change turnaround time
- Security incident response time
- Audit completeness

## 2. Secondary Stakeholders

**Their Needs:**

- Performance ranking within network

## 4. Stakeholder Engagement Strategy

### High Priority - Continuous Engagement
- **Customers**: User testing, feedback surveys, support tickets
- **Lab Operators**: Interface usability testing, production trial
- **Operations Manager**: Daily monitoring, intervention workflows

### Medium Priority - Regular Check-ins
- **Lab Managers**: Monthly performance reviews, feedback sessions
- **Hub Staff**: Weekly operations meetings, process improvements
- **Platform Administrator**: Configuration validation, security reviews

### Low Priority - Milestone-Based
- **Academic Supervisor**: Sprint reviews, milestone presentations
- **Examining Panel**: Final demonstration and defense
- **Business Owner**: Requirements validation, scope adjustments

---

## 5. Stakeholder Conflict Areas

### Potential Conflicts:

1. **Customer vs. Lab**: Quality disputes, deadline pressures
   - *Resolution*: Central hub inspection, fault attribution

2. **Lab vs. Platform**: Fair work distribution, pricing conflicts
   - *Resolution*: Transparent performance ledger, configurable exploration allowance

3. **Operations vs. Algorithms**: Manual overrides vs. automated decisions
   - *Resolution*: Audit trail, justification requirements

4. **Speed vs. Quality**: Fast quotes vs. accurate schedules
   - *Resolution*: Asynchronous quoting, bounded time budgets

5. **Academic vs. Practical**: Scope ambition vs. 6-month timeline
   - *Resolution*: Clearly defined MVP vs. extended scope

## Summary

The PrintGrid platform serves a **complex stakeholder ecosystem** with often competing needs. Success depends on:

✅ **Balancing** customer promises with lab capabilities  
✅ **Ensuring fairness** across labs of different sizes  
✅ **Maintaining quality** without direct control over production  
✅ **Providing transparency** while protecting intellectual property  
✅ **Scaling operations** while keeping human oversight where it matters
